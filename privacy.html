Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 25, 2026

The short version. Your tennis data — tournaments, results, history, notes, ratings — stays on your computer. AceLens doesn't sell or share anything you give us, and there's no advertising or tracking.

We receive only a few things, listed below: what you enter when you download or ask for an activation code, a once-a-day check-in from the app, and the optional emails and live calendar if you switch them on.

This policy covers the AceLens website (acelens.vercel.app) and the AceLens app for Windows and Mac. "AceLens", "we" and "us" mean the people who make and sell AceLens. Questions: acelens.app@gmail.com.

1. Your tennis data stays on your computer

AceLens keeps everything it shows you in a folder on your own computer. When it reads public pages — USTA tournaments and standings, UTR ratings, US National Weather Service and NOAA forecasts — the request goes straight from your computer to that site; it doesn't pass through us.

Where a site needs you to be signed in (your USTA or UTR account), you sign in inside a window in AceLens on your own computer. We never see those passwords, and AceLens only reads.

2. What we receive, and why

When you download AceLens from the website

Before a download you sign in with Google (see below), so we can help with setup and tell you about important updates. We keep:

  • Your name and email address, from your Google sign-in.
  • Optional answers: whether you're a parent, player, coach or club; the player's age group; your state; how you heard about AceLens.
  • Whether you'd like news and tips by email (only if you tick the box), and whether you downloaded for Windows or Mac.

Signing in with Google

When you choose "Continue with Google", Google tells us your name, your email address and that the email is verified. We check with Google that the sign-in is genuine, save your name and email as your download sign-up, and keep nothing else from your Google account — no password, no contacts, no access to your Gmail, Drive or anything else. We only ask Google for the basic sign-in information.

AceLens's use of information received from Google follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements.

When you ask for an activation code

Your name, email, the machine code AceLens shows for your computer (a code made from the computer, containing nothing personal), any note you add, and your computer type and AceLens version — so we can send a code that works on that computer.

The app's daily check-in

Once a day, the app tells AceLens's service its machine code, its version, the computer type (Windows or Mac) and whether it's activated — plus the name and email you gave if you asked for a code. This makes the free trial, activation and updates work, and lets us help when something goes wrong. It contains no tennis data.

Optional emails you switch on

If you turn on the tournament-morning email or the draw-posted alert, the email — the tournament, venue, weather, and your draw or next match — and the addresses you chose go through AceLens's mail sender, which sends it to those addresses (at most 3 emails a day per computer). Turn the emails off and nothing more is sent.

The optional live calendar

If you switch on the live calendar (so Google Calendar or Outlook can show your tournaments), your tournament list — names, dates and places — is kept on AceLens's calendar service at a private link only your calendar knows. Switching it off deletes it.

When you email us

What you write and your email address, to answer you.

3. How we use it

  • To give you the download, the free trial, activation codes and updates.
  • To help with setup and problems, and to tell you about important changes to AceLens.
  • To send the emails and calendar you switched on.
  • To send news and tips — only if you asked for them. Say "stop" any time.

We never sell your information, never share it for advertising, and never use it to build AI models.

4. Who helps us run AceLens

  • Google — AceLens's sign-ups, code requests, mail sender and calendar service run in AceLens's own Google account (Google Sheets and Apps Script). "Continue with Google" is provided by Google, under Google's Privacy Policy. The website's fonts come from Google Fonts.
  • Vercel hosts this website and keeps standard server logs (such as IP addresses) for a short time.
  • GitHub stores the installers and updates; your download and update checks come from GitHub's servers.

5. Cookies and browser storage

The website uses no tracking or advertising cookies and no analytics. It keeps one small note in your browser after you sign up, so you aren't asked again on that browser. If you use "Continue with Google", Google's sign-in may use its own cookies.

6. Keeping and deleting your information

We keep your sign-up and activation details while you use AceLens, so we can support you. Email acelens.app@gmail.com to see what we have, correct it, or have it deleted. Data in the app is yours: delete the AceLens folder, or uninstall, and it's gone from your computer. You can also remove AceLens from your Google account at myaccount.google.com/connections.

7. Children

AceLens is made for junior tennis families and is meant to be downloaded and set up by a parent or guardian. If you're under 13, please ask a parent to download AceLens and sign up for you. If you think a child under 13 gave us their details, email us and we'll delete them.

8. Security

Your tennis data never leaves your computer unless you switch on an email or the live calendar. What we receive is kept in AceLens's Google account, which only AceLens can access, and is sent over encrypted connections.

9. Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update this page and the date at the top. Important changes will also be announced in the app or by email.
